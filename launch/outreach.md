# Outreach

Rules for every message:

- One person, one message, about something they actually posted. No mass DMs, no copy-paste blasts.
- Follow each platform's rules on direct messages and self-promotion. If a group bans DMs about services, don't send them.
- Never claim clients, results, years of experience or credentials you don't have. Never call yourself a CPA, tax preparer or "tax" anything.
- Always say it's bookkeeping, not tax prep.

**Timing note (late November 2026):** the 2026 year isn't over yet. December statements usually arrive in early January, and PM 1099s by the end of January. Until then you are taking bookings, not documents. Say so plainly, so nobody pays expecting a workbook next week.

## 0. Before keyclosebooks.com is live

You can start conversations before the domain, checkout or form exist, because the first message only offers a sample. Until the domain works:

- **Share the sample PDF as an attachment** (`site/sample-rental-books-2026.pdf`), or via `https://<project>.pages.dev/sample-rental-books-2026.pdf` once Cloudflare Pages is set up (DEPLOY.md Step 2; this needs no domain). Don't send a keyclosebooks.com link yet: it won't open.
- **Don't publish hello@keyclosebooks.com** until Email Routing works (DEPLOY.md Step 4b), or replies will bounce. Reply from your own email meanwhile.
- **Don't take money** until Lemon Squeezy is approved and payouts are confirmed. If someone says yes, tell them you'll send the checkout link within a day or two, and that documents are needed in January once December's statement is out.
- **Cold email** (emailing people who haven't contacted you) to US recipients falls under the CAN-SPAM Act: a truthful subject line, your physical postal address, and a clear way to opt out, honoured promptly. Replying to someone's public post, or emailing someone who wrote to you first, is the safer start.

## 4. BiggerPockets: value-first public reply (no pitch)

The forums moderate promotion. This reply contains **no link, no price and no offer**: it answers the question. If someone asks what you do, answer only that.

> A few things that make PM statements much easier for your CPA, whoever does the work:
>
> 1. **Ask your PM for gross numbers, not just the net payout.** Your CPA needs gross rent collected, and each fee separately (management fee, leasing fee, repairs), not only the amount that hit your bank.
> 2. **Tie each month's payout to your bank.** Put the statement's net distribution next to the deposit. Any month that doesn't match is usually a reserve the PM held back, a late deposit or a missing statement, and it's much easier to sort out now than in March.
> 3. **Check for all 12 months per property.** Missing months are the most common gap. Ask the PM now, while someone still remembers.
> 4. **Keep a separate list of anything you paid yourself** (insurance, a repair you paid directly, HOA). It isn't on the PM statement, so it's easy to forget.
> 5. **Ask your PM which contractors they issued 1099s to.** Then you and your CPA can see whether anything is left for you to handle.
>
> Your CPA will make the calls on how things are reported. These steps just give them clean inputs.

Adapt the wording to each thread. Don't paste the same block everywhere, because that reads as spam and moderators notice.
